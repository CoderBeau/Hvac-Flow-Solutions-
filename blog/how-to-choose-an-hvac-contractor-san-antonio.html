Why comparing three quotes matters here

San Antonio summers push AC systems to their limit, and when a unit dies in July, panic pricing is real. Getting three competing quotes is the single easiest way to protect yourself—it exposes both the overpriced bids and the suspiciously cheap ones, and it gives you leverage to negotiate.

The 7 things to check before you hire

  • Texas license (TACLA/TACLB). Every legitimate HVAC contractor in Texas is licensed through TDLR. Ask for the license number and verify it—no number, no deal.
  • Insurance. They should carry general liability and workers' comp. If someone is hurt on your property and the company isn't insured, that can land on you.
  • Bexar County permit. AC replacements and new installs require a mechanical permit and inspection. A pro who offers to skip it to "save you money" is cutting a corner you'll pay for later.
  • Manual J sizing. The right system size comes from a load calculation based on your home's square footage, insulation, windows, and orientation—not a guess off the old unit. Oversized systems short-cycle and cost more to run.
  • Written, itemized estimate. Equipment model, labor, permit fees, and warranty should all be on paper before any work starts. Verbal-only quotes invite surprise charges.
  • Recent, real reviews. Look for a steady stream of recent Google reviews from the San Antonio area, and check how the company responds to criticism.
  • Written warranty. Know what's covered on parts and labor, and for how long. Reputable installers stand behind both.

Red flags to walk away from

  • High-pressure, "today only" pricing or door-to-door pitches
  • No license number, or a refusal to provide one
  • Cash-only, large deposit up front, no written contract
  • Offers to skip the permit or inspection
  • A bid dramatically lower than every other quote—usually a sign of undersized equipment or hidden add-ons

San Antonio–specific things most guides miss

Sizing for our heat. A system sized for a milder climate will struggle through a South Texas August. Make sure the load calculation accounts for attic heat gain and real square footage—an undersized unit runs constantly and drives up your CPS Energy bill.

Hard water and coils. San Antonio's hard water leaves mineral scale that can shorten equipment life. Ask contractors how they protect condensate lines and coils during service.

Freeze readiness. After the 2021 winter storm, heating matters here too. A good pro will talk about heat performance and emergency heat, not just cooling.

Contractors who bring these up unprompted know the San Antonio market—that local knowledge is exactly what separates a trustworthy pro from an out-of-town lead reseller.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit to replace my AC in San Antonio?

Yes. In San Antonio and Bexar County, HVAC replacements and new installations require a mechanical permit, and the work should be done by a Texas-licensed (TACLA/TACLB) contractor. A pro who skips the permit is a red flag—unpermitted work can fail inspection and cause problems when you sell the home.
